                  How the numbers are made

                  Cost of living is a price level against the United States: the World Bank PPP conversion factor divided by the official exchange rate, held inside a planning band when dual exchange rates would distort it. The monthly budget starts from a mid-tier US basket, then scales by that price level, the lifestyle you pick, and household size. Another adult counts as about two-thirds of a basket, a child about two-fifths, and housing grows more slowly than the rest.

                  Travel uses daily lodging, food, and local rates on the same price level, plus a visa-fee ballpark, paperwork, and a flight from the distance between capitals. A permanent move is a one-time relocation â€” deposit, setup, flights, papers â€” plus the monthly budget.

                  Salaries are indicative formal-sector averages, with a low-to-high band at roughly two-thirds to one-and-two-thirds of the average. Net pay uses an approximate tax and social wedge. The living rate on the economy tab compares typical take-home pay with a decent one-person budget. It is not the household budget from the monthly tab. The general job figure blends unemployment, growth, and a mild drag from very low pay. It is not a score for a specific profession.
